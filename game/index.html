Snakes and Ladders:
The Destiny Game

Shaping fairer futures

Everyone rolls the same die. What differs is the board society has built for each of us. Based on the Human-Centric Development Model (HCDM).

The big idea

The Human-Centric Development Model (HCDM) asks one question: does a society turn what it can produce into good lives for everyone? It scores the answer as Inclusive Prosperity, from 0 to 1, by looking at five parts. Each has its own colour on the board:

  • Productive capacity (orange): technology, roads, power and a healthy environment.
  • Basic needs (blue): water, energy, food and shelter. The weakest one caps everyone.
  • Human capital (green): education and health. Basic needs and human capital together make wellbeing.
  • Fair shares & inclusion (purple): how fairly gains are shared, and who can take part in work and markets.
  • Social fabric (pink): trust, fair institutions and belonging. It multiplies everything.

The dice is chance. Society shapes the board. Society decides how many ladders and snakes there are, how long they are, and who can reach them. This game lets you feel that.

How to play

  1. You are dealt a hand of 8 cards: 4 give you access to ladders (your benefits) and 4 protect you from snakes. The other 4 snakes are your drawbacks: you have increased vulnerability to them. It is the hand you’ve been dealt, and it stays on screen as you play.
  2. Roll and move. That is your only action. Your cards play themselves.
  3. Ladders: land at the foot of one. If you have access (its card is in your hand), you climb. If not, you can’t access that ladder because of the hand you were dealt, and you stay put.
  4. Snakes: land on a head. If you’re protected, nothing happens. If you have increased vulnerability, you slide down, but only half as far if you hold Trust & community.
  5. First to reach 100 wins: a thriving life, and a sign of what shared progress looks like.
Players

Pass the device round. Set a seat to Computer to play on your own.

What is the HCDM?

The Human-Centric Development Model, developed by Dr Marius Oosthuizen, measures Inclusive Prosperity (P) on a 0–1 scale: whether a society turns its productive capacity into broadly shared human flourishing.

P = √(Pr·Wb) × √(D·In) × Φ(π)
  • Productive capacity (Pr): technology, infrastructure and a healthy ecology.
  • Wellbeing (Wb): basic needs (the weakest one caps everyone) turned into capability by human capital, education and health.
  • Fair shares & inclusion (D, In): how fairly gains are shared and how widely people take part.
  • Social fabric (π): trust, legitimacy, agency, hope and cohesion. It multiplies everything.

The dice is chance, but society decides how many ladders and snakes there are. The HCDM helps decision-makers build more and stronger ladders, and shorten or block the snakes, so that everyone gets to play.

The “conditions score” above applies the v2 formula to each hand as an illustration. It is not real-world data.
