The name

The i stands for intelligence

The name is still spoken CUSP. The small, lowered i stands for intelligence: a mark of a fundamental shift, and a core commitment to use AI across all of this work — keynotes, strategic foresight, scenario planning, anticipatory governance advisory, facilitation, executive teaching and research.

Superintelligence is arriving, and human-centric institutions in government and business will be fundamentally challenged by it; lagging in adoption does not keep an institution safe, it only means others shape the technology for you. The answer is to operate at the frontier as an early adopter, while keeping a critically reflective posture on the ethical implications of its use — bias and fairness, the concentration of power, privacy, labour and dignity, and whether Africa and the Global South are left behind.

Use AI boldly; question it relentlessly. Boldness without reflection is reckless; reflection without adoption is irrelevant.

About

Helping leaders and institutions anticipate and shape fairer futures

Dr Marius Oosthuizen is a South African futurist, systems transformation architect and specialist in strategic foresight and anticipatory governance, based in Pretoria.

His North Star is to help leaders and institutions anticipate and shape fairer futures through ethical foresight, systems thinking and inclusive dialogue. One question guides the work: how do societies intentionally shape systems towards fair, inclusive and sustainable futures?

His work is about building the capability to see ahead: reading weak signals, developing scenarios and turning foresight into better strategy and policy. He helps strategic leaders in government, business and civil society adapt to complex challenges, combining ethical awareness with future-oriented strategy. Through CUSiP, his practice for foresight, keynotes, facilitation, teaching and research, he has made a core commitment to use AI across all of this work, with a focus on human-centric systems change in Africa, and particular attention to energy transitions, infrastructure and development.

Marius is Professor of Foresight at the Future Readiness Academy (Abu Dhabi) and serves on the co-chairs committee guiding the World Energy Council's global scenarios. He is the foresight consultant on the United Nations Population Fund's (UNFPA) Six Country Demographic Resilience Review Study, and works with the United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs (UN DESA) and its Project Office on Governance (UNPOG) on strategic foresight and AI for the public sector.

Earlier, he was Strategic Foresight Expert with UN Global Pulse in the Executive Office of the Secretary-General (2024–2025), where as lead faculty of Foresight for Systems Transformation he trained more than 300 people across UN agencies and built foresight capability for the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office. He supported the Government of Indonesia on its sea-level rise resilience policy, was Director, Scenarios at the World Energy Council (2022–2023), Director, Learning at the School of International Futures (2021–2023), and faculty at the Gordon Institute of Business Science (GIBS) at the University of Pretoria (2014–2022), where Executive MBA students voted him Best Lecturer in 2018.

The CUSiP approach

Six practices for shaping fairer futures

These practices now run with AI in the work: used boldly, at the frontier of practice, and questioned relentlessly, including their assumptions, blind spots and consequences.

A Systems Transformation Architect is “an integrative public leader who works across institutional, sectoral, and epistemic boundaries to help actors understand systemic conditions, imagine preferable futures, identify leverage points, build coalitions, design transformation processes, and mobilize coordinated interventions that shift the behavior, structure, and trajectory of complex systems.”

From The Systems Transformation Architect, Marius Oosthuizen (2026)

  1. Anticipate

    Sense change. Explore futures. Hold long horizons.

  2. Understand

    See systems. Diagnose root conditions. Find leverage.

  3. Convene

    Build trust across divides. Facilitate dialogue. Create shared purpose.

  4. Design

    Co-create strategies, policies and solutions that are practical and just.

  5. Transform

    Shift institutions and systems. Scale what works.

  6. Narrate

    Shape stories. Communicate truth. Inspire action.

At the centreHuman dignity. Justice. Common good. A flourishing future for all.

Across scalesLocal · National · Regional · Continental · Global, with multi-stakeholder coalitions

Measures of impact

  • Systems better aligned to justice and the common good
  • Institutions more adaptive and collaborative
  • Communities more empowered and resilient
  • Futures more hopeful than inherited

Two CUSiP tools

The cone of plausibility explores probable, plausible and possible futures, clarifies a preferred future, and draws a strategy line from the present towards it. From foresight to strategy turns that exploration into a resilient strategy response.

Publications and research

Selected publications

Practice-led research on foresight methods, anticipatory governance, the futures of Africa and South Africa, and how human-centric institutions can stay legitimate, accountable and humane in an AI-saturated world.

  • Practitioner-theory paper · May 2026

    The Systems Transformation Architect: An Emerging Professional Role for Integrative Public Leadership and Institutional Innovation

    Argues that a new professional role is emerging at the intersection of strategic foresight, systems thinking, public entrepreneurship, institutional innovation and multi-stakeholder governance: an integrative actor who helps institutions and coalitions sense systemic change, convene across boundaries, identify leverage points, shape narratives, design participatory processes and mobilise coordinated action toward preferable futures.

  • Working paper · 2026

    Waves of Consequence: Introducing the Event Horizon Contagion Method for Structured Pattern Recognition

    A multi-wave foresight method integrating Three Horizons, futures wheels, cross-impact analysis and morphological scenarios, with an applied case study of geopolitical and economic contagion.

  • Working paper · 2026

Speaking topics

Signature talks

Each talk is tailored to the audience, from boards and cabinets to conference plenaries.

Future Readiness: Using Strategic Foresight and Anticipation for Strategic Agility

Strategy fails less because of what leaders do not know than because of what they have not imagined. How foresight helps organisations read weak signals, stress-test plans and change course quickly.

Anticipatory Governance: Building the State's Capacity to See Ahead

Drawing on work with UN and African institutions: how a public service, or a whole country, can build a lasting foresight and scenario capability that informs policy and budgets.

AI, Foresight and the Future of the Public Sector

What superintelligence and artificial intelligence mean for how governments decide, serve citizens and manage risk, and how foresight helps leaders govern AI wisely rather than simply react to it, or lag while others shape the technology for them.

Power and Compute: The Energy–AI Intersection

The AI boom is also an energy story. Drawing on the World Energy Council's global scenarios: how the two transitions are converging, and what it means for business and policy in Africa.

Africa's Futures: Scenarios for South Africa and the Continent

Demography, technology, governance and a shifting world order: a clear, scenario-based view of where opportunity and risk lie for leaders and investors.

Leading Under Uncertainty: Foresight for Leaders and Teams

Practical foresight tools for executives and emerging leaders who must decide with incomplete information, drawn from leadership development faculty work.
